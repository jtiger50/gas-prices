Marylanders have spent $2 billion more on gas since Iran war began

By Jake Tiger

Perhaps the biggest reason for the midterm election’s blue tsunami was green.

Affordability was top of mind for voters casting their midterm ballots on Nov. 3, with many feeling that decisions by President Donald Trump — from tariffs to the war in Iran — had taken money out of their pockets.

A Reuters/Ipsos poll on Election Day found that cost of living was the most important factor for 48% of voters, making it the top issue by far.

Nowhere were voters’ financial frustrations fueled more than at the gas pump, where Americans have watched costs count up and up after the first strikes on Iran.

As of mid-October, Marylanders had spent an additional $2 billion on gas since the Iran war began in February, equating to $777 per family, according to an October report from the U.S. Senate Joint Economic Committee’s minority arm.

As of Nov. 12, regular gas prices were up 25.5% statewide from a year ago, according to data from AAA. Costs recently fell back below $4 a gallon, despite U.S. strikes on Iran as recently as Nov. 2.

The Cumberland metro area was hit the hardest, with gas costing 33% more than the same time last year. Baltimore and Hagerstown were the least affected at 24.1% each, AAA data showed.

Maryland ranks 41st among states by per-family additional spending on gas at $777. That’s about even with West Virginia, while Virginia and Delaware each land above $930. Nationally, Americans have collectively spent $101 billion more on gas since February, according to the Joint Economic Committee minority report.

U.S. gas costs were relatively steady or declining through the first year of Trump’s second term. In January, just a month before the war began, the average price for all grades had fallen to $2.94 per gallon, according to the U.S. Energy Information Administration. It was the lowest mark for fuel prices since March 2021, when the nation was still emerging from a pandemic that suppressed demand.
